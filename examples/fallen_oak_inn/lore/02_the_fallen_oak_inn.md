# The Fallen Oak Inn

The Fallen Oak is the only shelter between Baldur's Gate and Beregost
that a traveller will not regret sleeping in. It stands at the
north-eastern edge of the Wood of Sharp Teeth, a three-day ride out of
Baldur's Gate on the Trade Way. It takes its name from the enormous
oak that came down in the Storm of the Unstrung Harp (1371 DR); the
trunk still lies across the innyard and serves as the inn's long bench.

The inn itself is two stories of sturdy wood and field-stone, the
second floor let out as six small rooms. Downstairs is the taproom — a
long low hall with a hearth at one end and the bar at the other, and
the fallen trunk propped under the window for the heavy drinkers. The
stables across the yard can hold eight horses. The privy is behind the
stables. The well is good. The ale is mediocre. The bread is bought
daily from a farm half a mile into the woods.

## The innkeeper

**Shea Raventongue** has run the Fallen Oak for nine years. She is
Illuskan, widowed, and the second most dangerous person anyone in
the building has ever met, though she does not volunteer that fact.
Her dead husband was a Flaming Fist captain who died during the Iron
Throne affair in Baldur's Gate. She keeps his sword mounted over the
bar. Nobody has asked to take it down.

Shea does not ask her guests questions they would have to lie about.
She keeps three rules — coin in advance, no blood on the floor, rooms
are locked by dark — and enforces them with a voice so even that it
takes new arrivals a full evening to register how frightened her
regulars are of her.

## The night the story begins

It is the first evening of Mirtul (The Melting), 1374 DR — roughly
mid-May. A three-day lightning storm has rolled in off the Sea of
Swords. The taproom is close, steaming, smelling of wet wool and
hearth-smoke. Four travellers besides Shea are sheltering here
tonight. None of them came together. None of them want to talk about
why they are on the road.

One week ago, a cart was found abandoned on the Trade Way two miles
south of the inn. Its driver had been burned to char in a dragon
attack — common enough — but the cart itself was strange: Thayan
script carved into the axle, and the strongbox under the driver's
seat was empty, its lid torn off from outside. Whoever took what was
in it walked north, toward the inn. The tracks ended where this
week's rain began.

That was six nights ago. Shea has said nothing about it. She is
watching tonight's guests more than she is watching the bar.

## What the player character is doing here

You are a travelling sword-for-hire. Three days ago, a Harper agent in
Baldur's Gate paid you half a purse of gold to go north and find out
what was taken from the Thayan cart, and who took it. The other half
comes if you return with the answer. The agent was specific: **you are
not to recover the object yourself.** If you find it, you leave it
where it is and you walk out.

The Harper — a white-haired half-elven woman who named herself
Mallara Thistledown — said one more thing before she handed you the
coin:

> *"Don't let the Red Wizard see you. Don't let the Zhent know you're
> asking. And don't pity the carrier; the carrier is the cursed one."*

Tonight you arrived at the Fallen Oak, ahead of the storm by perhaps
an hour.
