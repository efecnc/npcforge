# Tone, register, canonical terms

## Tone

Low fantasy. Rain. Hearth-smoke. People who have seen something
burn and have not yet agreed to talk about it. Not heroic; never
cinematic. Funny only when scared. Violence, when it comes, is
fast and off-page.

## Register

Most characters at the Fallen Oak speak **working-class Sword Coast
Common** — short sentences, plain vocabulary, the occasional
inherited idiom ("by the cold moon", "if the road holds"). The Red
Wizard speaks **college** register — Mulhorandi-inflected, formal,
archly polite. The Harper speaks **high school** — warm on the
surface, careful underneath. The Zhent is a working-class liar who
uses bigger words when nervous.

## Canonical terms

- Currency is **gold pieces**, **silver pieces**, **copper pieces**,
  or the northern **Amnian trade-bar**.
- The great road is always **the Trade Way**, never "the highway"
  or "the route".
- Dragon attacks are **dragonflight** (collective) or **a wyrm came
  down** (singular).
- The recent crisis is **the Rage of Dragons** (historical) or
  **the Rage** (colloquial). Never "the dragon war".
- The Zhentarim are **Zhents** in speech, never "the network".
- Thayans are **Red Wizards** (capital-R when the speaker is polite,
  lowercase otherwise) or **Thayans**. Never just "wizards" in
  contexts where the faction matters.
- The Harpers are never self-named; they refer to their allies as
  **friends on the road**.
- Deities are named: **Tymora** for luck, **Lathander** for dawn /
  new beginnings, **Mask** for thieves, **Bane** (dead, returned)
  for tyranny, **Cyric** for lies, **Kelemvor** for the dead. Oaths
  by any of these land differently on the hearers.
- **Candlekeep** is the great library at the south end of the Sword
  Coast. A Candlekeep monk does not lie about small things.

## Forbidden register anywhere in this setting

- Modern: "computer", "internet", "car", "phone", "LOL", "dude",
  "awesome", "cool", "chill", "vibe".
- Anachronistic fantasy: "doth", "thee", "thou", "verily",
  "hitherto", "forsooth" — Faerûn Common is early modern, not
  Shakespearean.

## Rating target

T — implied violence (dragon attacks off-page, burned bodies
mentioned), implied lethality, no explicit gore, no sex, mild
profanity allowed ("damn", "hell", "bloody"). Nothing harder.
